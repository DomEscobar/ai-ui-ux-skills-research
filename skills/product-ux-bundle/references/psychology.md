# Use psychology as a hypothesis, not a persuasion script

| Trigger | Design action | Check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Interrupted mobile or long workflow | Preserve draft, position and next step; separate optional notifications from task-critical events | Interrupt, return and finish without repeating entry |
| Users must remember earlier information or distinguish similar states | Keep important context visible; label actions in user language and use the same term for the same state | New user explains what happened and what comes next |
| High-stakes action or plausible AI mistake | Show evidence/source, preview, correction/undo when possible, and a human fallback when appropriate | Test a plausible wrong suggestion and a correct one; observe whether both are handled appropriately |
| Low-stakes repeatable task | Reduce redundant steps; keep safety and accessibility; do not add confirmation by default | Measure errors and effort, not just clicks |
| Feed, autoplay, reminders, subscription or consent | Show user controls at the point of need, respect pause/exit, and avoid punitive friction, fake scarcity, hidden refusal or guilt | Compare subscribe/cancel and allow/refuse paths; track intended task success separately from usage time |
| Targeting an age group or vulnerable audience | Validate in the relevant context; segment by task, access and familiarity. Add privacy and safety protections appropriate to the audience and product | Observe representative users; do not infer abilities or behaviors from age or generation labels |

**Evidence hygiene:** A population survey describes use, not causal cognitive change. A time-limited experiment is not evidence of permanent effects. A group mean does not describe every person. Do not defend a pattern with an “eight-second attention span,” an invented dopamine story, or an anecdote about Gen Z. For a trade-off, state the intended benefit and plausible burden; measure both, separately from session duration.

Calibration: [W3C cognitive usability](https://www.w3.org/TR/coga-usable/) is guidance; [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) is a standard. [Buçinca et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2102.09692) test AI overreliance reduction in a particular task, not universal mandatory friction. [Orben et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-29296-3) report heterogeneous longitudinal associations, not causation.
